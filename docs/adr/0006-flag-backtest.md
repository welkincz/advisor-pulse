# ADR 0006 — Backtest the report's flags against the generator's ground truth

- Status: Proposed (threshold change waits for the owner's OK on PLAN.md §7)
- Date: 2026-10-23
- Issues: #300 (rules from #268)

## Context

The weekly report exists to catch problems early, so a manager acts sooner. Until now
nothing measured whether its flags do. Real data never says which changes were real. The
synthetic generator does, because it injects them:

- **District volume.** From 2026-W34 the falling story district (BCI) loses 5% of its
  activity a week and the rising one (BCL) gains 5%, reaching ±35% in 2026-W40.
- **Portfolio drift.** 12% of advisors, chosen by the seed, concentrate on fewer clients
  from November 2025, over 270 days, so their portfolios' 12-month contacted rate slides.
- **Calendar.** Holiday weeks and the February lift change volume by design.

## Decision

### Ground truth

`pulse.generate.truth` derives every story from the seed with the generator's own
functions (`story_factor`, `is_drifter`, refactored out of `activity.py` with identical
output). It writes `ground_truth.json` beside the data and never into `sources/`, so the
generated bytes do not change. Org records summarize each affected node and story; advisor records identify the seeded
source events (volume follows assignment ranges; drift follows the advisor). Advisor
events are exported but not scored, because `mart_weekly_report` has no advisor flag rows.
Only events whose detection window intersects the observed history enter the export;
planned future stories do not count as missed detections.
For each record: level, node,
direction, the measures it moves directly and those it moves slightly, the first changed
week, the full-size week, the last week a flag can be credited to it, and its size.

- **Volume** stories: the advisor-weighted mean story factor at full size, minus one.
  Scored when it is at least 5%: the 17 staffed branches of BCI and BCL, both districts
  and the BC region (+11% on the tuning seed, +14% on the held-out one). Canada nets out at +1.8%, so it is not a story. Window: the
  first changed week to 13 weeks after full size, when the baseline has absorbed it.
  Measures: activities per advisor, outbound calls, appointments booked, unique clients.
- **Drift** stories: the share of a node's portfolio clients held by a drifter when the
  ramp ends, minus the bank's share. Scored when it is at least 10 points: 27 branches,
  the one-branch PEI district and its PE region on the tuning seed; 26 branches and the
  BCI district on the held-out one. Window:
  the first changed week to a year after full size, since a 12-month rate keeps sliding
  until a whole year has rolled over. Measure: portfolio contacted (12 months).

### Scoring

`pulse.quality.backtest` reruns the mart's flag rules in DuckDB with the thresholds as
parameters. At the shipped thresholds the result must equal `mart_weekly_report.flag` on
every row, or the command stops. That ties the backtest to the flags the site shows, and
other thresholds can be tried without rebuilding dbt. For each rule and level:

- a flag is a **hit** when it points the way of a scored story at its node, on a measure
  the story moves, inside its window;
- it is **explained** when it directly matches a smaller story: it counts toward precision
  but not recall. An indirect overlap on another measure is not proof and gets no credit;
- every other flag is a **false alarm**. Those in calendar weeks are counted separately
  but still count as false alarms, because a manager cannot act on a holiday.
- **Precision** is (hits + directly explained flags) divided by **all flags**. **Recall** is the share of scored
  stories with at least one hit. **Lag** is the median number of weeks from the first
  changed week to the first hit. **False alarms** are per 100 node-weeks of that level,
  across every measure the rule watches. DOWN and UP are scored separately, with only
  stories in the same direction eligible for recall. PRTF anomaly direction means a
  stronger/weaker move than its historical mean, not necessarily an absolute rise/fall.

All twelve KPIs are included, including LATE_RATE and QUAR_RATE at national level. The
regular defect injections are background, not labelled operational rate-change stories:
operational recall is unassessed and their alerts count as unmatched. The catch-up load
can differ from sequential weekly loads on these per-batch rates; this is an evaluation
of these two built warehouses, not proof of operational alert sensitivity.

### Tuning and the held-out seed

The thresholds were tuned on seed 20261013, the one the site runs on, and confirmed on
seed 424242, which is not an input to the tuning function. The proposed selection
budget is **25 false alarms per 100 branch-weeks** across both alerting rules,
about one a month per branch across every measure; this is an evaluation assumption,
not measured manager capacity. Within that budget, catch the most
stories, then the shortest lags, then the fewest false alarms. Grid: z ∈ {2, 2.5, 3, 3.5,
4}; move z ∈ {1.5, 2, 2.5, 3} × minimum move ∈ {0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.5} points.

| | Before | Chosen |
|---|---|---|
| Activity anomaly | \|z\| > 2 against the 13 weeks before | **\|z\| > 3** |
| Portfolio contacted move | move \|z\| > 2 and ≥ 0.5 points | move \|z\| > 2 and **≥ 0.2 points** |
| UP / DOWN | ≥ 1% week on week | unchanged, not an alert |

What the held-out seed says:

- **The anomaly flag now raises a quarter of the false alarms**, 70 → 17 per 100
  branch-weeks, on both seeds. The cost shows only on the held-out seed: it misses 4 of
  17 changed branches, and the median lag goes from 2 to 4 weeks. Every district and the
  region are still caught. A manager watching a district still sees the story.
- **The contacted-move rule catches far more drifting portfolios**, 73% → 96% of branches
  on the held-out seed, for about 3 more false alarms per 100 branch-weeks. The lag is
  long (about 3 months) because a 12-month rate moves slowly; the tested threshold grid did not
  find a faster combination under the stated false-alarm budget.
- **UP and DOWN are not alerts.** DOWN alone produces about 389 unmatched flags per
  100 branch-weeks on the held-out seed, and catches only 7 of 31 downward stories.
  The site uses these labels to describe the week, not to alert. Anomaly precision
  at branch level is only 1.3%, even though recall is 13/17: the tuned rule is still
  noisy, not a validated detector for a real bank.
- **Holidays cause most national false alarms** (20 of 24 on the held-out seed). DQ031
  already divides volume by the calendar's expectation. Doing the same in the anomaly
  baseline is tracked in [#332](https://github.com/welkincz/advisor-pulse/issues/332)
  and still needs its own backtest.

### Guarding it

`tests/quality/test_backtest_minimums.py` (slow, about 35 minutes) builds a default-scale
warehouse over 2026-W14 to 2026-W42 plus the 52-week warm-up, which holds both stories,
and pins the proposed regression minimums there (the current run passed in 30 min 26 s):

| Rule | Level | Minimum recall | Maximum lag | Maximum false / 100 node-weeks | Measured |
|---|---|--:|--:|--:|---|
| Anomaly | District | 100% | 2 wk | 25 | 100%, 1 wk, 17.04 |
| Anomaly | Branch | 90% | 3 wk | 25 | 100%, 2 wk, 14.81 |
| Contacted move | Branch | 60% | 40 wk | 10 | 63%, 38 wk, 4.07 |

The shorter window starts the report 23 weeks after the drift began, so the
contacted-move lag is longer there than over the full history.

## How to reproduce

Each seed takes about 4 minutes to generate and 45 minutes for one catch-up run on an
Apple M4 Max. Use a scratch data dir and never set `R2_*`:

```bash
export PULSE_SEED=424242 PULSE_DATA_DIR=/tmp/pulse-424242/data PULSE_EXPORT_DIR=/tmp/pulse-424242/export
uv run pulse generate --backfill --from 2024-W41 --to 2026-W42
uv run pulse run --week 2026-W42 --run-type backfill    # one run loads every week
make backtest BACKTEST_DIRS="/tmp/pulse-20261013/data /tmp/pulse-424242/data" ARGS=--tune
```

`ARGS='--web web/src/features/quality/backtestResults.ts'` rewrites the table on
`/quality`. One catch-up run builds the same marts as 106 weekly runs, except the
per-batch LATE_RATE and QUAR_RATE; these remain in the backtest with the limitation above.

## Consequences

- The proposed thresholds reduce unmatched branch anomalies by about three quarters.
  They still generate many false alarms and miss four of seventeen changed branches
  on the held-out seed. A small branch change can take a few weeks longer to flag.
- The thresholds are a measured choice, and any later change can be scored with one
  command. The slow test stops a change that silently makes detection worse.
- The scores are only as good as the synthetic stories. Real changes are messier, so
  these numbers show the method works, not how it would do at a real bank.
